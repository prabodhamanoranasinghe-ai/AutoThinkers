17:T1b2a,<p>Most people do not need twenty AI apps. They need a <strong>small toolkit</strong> that reliably removes shallow work.</p>
<p>This AutoThinkers guide is for creators, founders, freelancers, and solo operators who want a practical AI stack — not a bookmark graveyard.</p>
<h2>The rule: one job per tool</h2>
<p>Before installing anything, assign jobs:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Job</th>
<th>Example outcome</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Think &#x26; draft</td>
<td>Briefs, outlines, first drafts</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Research assist</td>
<td>Summaries with sources to verify</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Repetitive ops</td>
<td>Email triage, meeting notes, formatting</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Make media</td>
<td>Thumbnails, diagrams, short clips</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Build software</td>
<td>Code help, refactors, tests</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If two tools share the same job, keep the one you open weekly.</p>
<h2>The recommended starter stack</h2>
<h3>1. Primary reasoning model (required)</h3>
<p>Pick <strong>one</strong> strong general model as your home base:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ChatGPT</strong> — broad ecosystem, strong everyday drafting</li>
<li><strong>Claude</strong> — excellent long-document work and careful rewriting</li>
<li><strong>Gemini</strong> — strong when your life already lives in Google Workspace</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Tutorial setup (15 minutes):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Create a Project / custom workspace named after your business</li>
<li>Upload only non-sensitive reference docs (brand voice, FAQ, offer sheet)</li>
<li>Save three reusable prompts: <em>Outline</em>, <em>Draft</em>, <em>Critique</em></li>
<li>Turn on memory features carefully — or keep a manual “context note” you paste</li>
</ol>
<h3>2. Notes system that stores prompts (required)</h3>
<p>AI without a notes home becomes chat chaos.</p>
<p>Use Notion, Obsidian, Apple Notes, or Google Docs — whatever you already trust.</p>
<p>Create four folders:</p>
<ol>
<li><code>Prompts</code></li>
<li><code>Workflows</code></li>
<li><code>Outputs to edit</code></li>
<li><code>Decisions</code></li>
</ol>
<h3>3. Transcription + meeting cleanup (high ROI)</h3>
<p>If you take calls, add a transcription tool (Otter, Granola, Fireflies, or built-in meeting notes).</p>
<p><strong>Workflow:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Record / transcribe</li>
<li>Paste transcript into your primary model</li>
<li>Ask for: decisions, open loops, customer language, next actions</li>
<li>File the cleaned note the same day</li>
</ol>
<h3>4. Optional image / media tool</h3>
<p>Add Midjourney, Ideogram, or Canva AI only if visuals are part of your offer.</p>
<p>Skip this layer if writing and ops are your bottleneck.</p>
<h3>5. Optional automation layer</h3>
<p>Zapier, Make, or n8n come <strong>after</strong> a manual workflow works twice.</p>
<p>Automating a broken process just creates expensive mess.</p>
<h2>One-afternoon setup tutorial</h2>
<h3>Hour 1 — Choose and configure</h3>
<ol>
<li>Pick primary model + notes app</li>
<li>Create your workspace</li>
<li>Write a 10-line “About my work” context block</li>
</ol>
<h3>Hour 2 — Build your first three prompts</h3>
<p><strong>Outline prompt</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: senior operator and clear writer.
Task: turn my rough notes into a structured outline.
Constraints: no fluff, keep my voice, mark assumptions.
Output: H2/H3 outline + open questions.
Notes:
{{paste}}
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Draft prompt</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-text">Expand this outline into a practical draft.
Audience: {{who}}
Tone: direct, specific, no hype.
Include examples and a short checklist at the end.
Outline:
{{outline}}
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Critique prompt</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-text">Critique this draft like a skeptical editor.
Flag: unclear claims, missing steps, weak CTA, generic advice.
Return: rewrite plan in 7 bullets, then a tighter intro.
Draft:
{{draft}}
</code></pre>
<h3>Hour 3 — Run a real task end-to-end</h3>
<p>Pick one unfinished piece of work:</p>
<ul>
<li>newsletter</li>
<li>landing page section</li>
<li>client proposal</li>
<li>SOP</li>
</ul>
<p>Run Outline → Draft → Critique → human edit → publish/send.</p>
<p>If that loop works, your toolkit is already valuable.</p>
<h2>What to avoid in 2026</h2>
<ul>
<li>Tool hopping every week</li>
<li>Paying for five “AI writing” apps that do the same thing</li>
<li>Pasting private customer data into random free tools</li>
<li>Publishing unedited AI text</li>
<li>Building automations before you have a checklist</li>
</ul>
<h2>A simple monthly review</h2>
<p>Once a month, ask:</p>
<ol>
<li>Which tool did I open most?</li>
<li>Which paid tool did I ignore?</li>
<li>Which prompt produced reusable output?</li>
<li>What still needs a human judgment step?</li>
</ol>
<p>Cancel ignored tools. Promote winning prompts into your notes system.</p>
<h2>Closing</h2>
<p>A practical AI toolkit is boring on purpose: one strong model, one notes home, one transcription habit, and optional media/automation later.</p>
<p>Master that stack before chasing the next launch. When you are ready for the next guide, we will go deeper on prompts, workflows, and tool-by-tool tutorials.</p>
