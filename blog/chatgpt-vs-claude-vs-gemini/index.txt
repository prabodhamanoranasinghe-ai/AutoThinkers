<p>Features and plans change often — treat this as an <strong>operator’s matrix</strong>, not a forever scoreboard. Re-test quarterly with your real tasks.</p>
<h2>ChatGPT: best general workhorse</h2>
<p><strong>Choose ChatGPT when you want:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>One tool for outlines, drafts, prompts, light research, and coding help</li>
<li>Custom GPTs / saved workflows for repeated jobs</li>
<li>Broad tutorials and community prompt patterns</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Watch outs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Long, dense rewrites can get generic unless you constrain tone</li>
<li>Easy to over-trust confident answers — always verify facts</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> creators, students, freelancers who need a daily default.</p>
<h2>Claude: best careful long-form partner</h2>
<p><strong>Choose Claude when you want:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Strong rewriting of essays, proposals, and SOPs</li>
<li>Thoughtful critique of your drafts</li>
<li>Comfortable handling longer pasted context</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Watch outs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Ecosystem extras may matter less if you only need chat</li>
<li>Still needs human fact-checking on citations and numbers</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> writers, researchers, consultants, students with heavy reading loads.</p>
<h2>Gemini: best if your life is in Google</h2>
<p><strong>Choose Gemini when you want:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Help inside Docs, Gmail, and Drive-centric workflows</li>
<li>Summaries tied to files you already store in Google</li>
<li>A model that matches a Google-first stack</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Watch outs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>If you live in Notion / local Markdown / Microsoft, the Workspace advantage shrinks</li>
<li>Same rule as others: verify before you publish or submit</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> students and teams already standardized on Google Workspace.</p>
<h2>How to decide in 20 minutes</h2>
<p>Run the <strong>same three prompts</strong> in each model you are considering:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Outline:</strong> “Create a practical tutorial outline on {{topic}} for beginners.”</li>
<li><strong>Rewrite:</strong> Paste 400 words of your own writing → “Tighten without changing my voice.”</li>
<li><strong>Work task:</strong> Paste a messy email/meeting note → “Extract actions with owners.”</li>
</ol>
<p>Score each 1–5 on: clarity, usefulness, and how much editing you still need.</p>
<p>Keep the winner as your default. Keep one runner-up free account for second opinions.</p>
<h2>Pricing mindset (not a price list)</h2>
<p>Plans change. Use this rule instead:</p>
<ul>
<li>Free tiers are for <strong>learning and light weekly use</strong></li>
<li>Paid plans are worth it when you use the tool <strong>most workdays</strong> and it saves real hours</li>
<li>Paying for two premium models is rarely step one — master one first</li>
</ul>
<p>If you are unsure about ChatGPT Plus specifically, pair this guide with a later “Is ChatGPT worth paying for?” post once you have usage data.</p>
<h2>Recommended stacks by audience</h2>
<h3>Students</h3>
<ul>
<li>Primary: ChatGPT <strong>or</strong> Claude</li>
<li>Optional: Gemini if notes live in Drive</li>
<li>Next reads: study systems + prompt libraries (linked below)</li>
</ul>
<h3>Small businesses</h3>
<ul>
<li>Primary: ChatGPT for drafts, FAQs, and sales emails</li>
<li>Claude for polishing proposals and policies</li>
<li>Keep customer data out of tools your policy does not allow</li>
</ul>
<h3>Solo operators / creators</h3>
<ul>
<li>Primary: one strong chat model</li>
<li>Add specialized tools later (image, transcription, automation) only after the chat loop is reliable</li>
</ul>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ol>
<li>Switching models every week (“tool hopping”)</li>
<li>Asking vague prompts and blaming the model</li>
<li>Publishing unedited AI text</li>
<li>Pasting confidential client data into consumer tools</li>
<li>Buying three paid plans before you have one reusable workflow</li>
</ol>
<h2>Closing checklist</h2>
<ul class="contains-task-list">
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> One primary model chosen</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Three test prompts saved</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> One real weekly workflow attached (study, email, or content)</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Fact-check rule in place</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Second model optional, not mandatory</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> ChatGPT is the safest default for most people, Claude shines on careful long-form work, and Gemini wins when Google Workspace is already home base.</p>
