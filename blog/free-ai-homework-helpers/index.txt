17:T2140,<p>Free AI homework helpers can save hours — or wreck your learning — depending on how you use them.</p>
<p>This guide focuses on tools and workflows that <strong>actually improve understanding</strong>, not just answer-dumping. If your school bans certain tools, follow that policy first.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Deep companion: <a href="/blog/ai-for-students-study-smarter/">AI for Students: Study Smarter…</a> · prompts: <a href="/blog/best-chatgpt-prompts-for-students/">Best ChatGPT Prompts for Students</a></p>
</blockquote>

<p>Free limits change — if a tool rate-limits you mid-semester, keep a backup.</p>
<h2>The rule that makes free tools “work”</h2>
<p>Use AI for this loop:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Explain</strong> the concept</li>
<li><strong>Practice</strong> without looking</li>
<li><strong>Check</strong> your attempt</li>
<li><strong>Retry</strong> the weak parts</li>
</ol>
<p>If you only paste the worksheet and copy the answer, the tool “worked” for one night and failed your exam.</p>
<h2>1) ChatGPT (free) — best all-purpose homework tutor</h2>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> explanations, practice questions, essay <em>outlines</em>, study plans<br>
<strong>Strengths:</strong> flexible prompts, good for almost every subject<br>
<strong>Watch outs:</strong> can invent facts; always verify formulas, dates, citations</p>
<p><strong>Tutor prompt (copy):</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: patient tutor.
Subject: {{subject}}
Topic: {{topic}}
My level: {{beginner/intermediate}}
Explain step-by-step, then give 5 practice questions.
Do not give full answers until I attempt them.
After I answer, score me and fix misunderstandings.
</code></pre>
<p>More prompt patterns: <a href="/blog/best-chatgpt-prompts-for-students/">Best ChatGPT Prompts for Students</a>.</p>
<h2>2) Claude (free tier) — best for long readings &#x26; careful feedback</h2>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> chapter summaries from <em>your</em> notes, feedback on essays you wrote<br>
<strong>Strengths:</strong> strong at long context and thoughtful edits<br>
<strong>Watch outs:</strong> free limits; still not a replacement for your own writing</p>
<p>Ask it to critique structure and clarity — not to ghostwrite the submission.</p>
<h2>3) Gemini (free) — best if your homework lives in Google</h2>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Docs/Drive-centric classes, brainstorming in Workspace<br>
<strong>Strengths:</strong> convenient when notes and assignments are already in Google<br>
<strong>Watch outs:</strong> same integrity rules as any chatbot</p>
<h2>4) Perplexity (free) — best research helper with sources</h2>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> background research, “what should I read?”, comparing explanations<br>
<strong>Strengths:</strong> source links you can open and verify<br>
<strong>Watch outs:</strong> links ≠ automatic truth — click through</p>
<p>When to use Perplexity vs ChatGPT: <a href="/blog/perplexity-vs-chatgpt/">Perplexity vs ChatGPT</a>.</p>
<h2>5) Math &#x26; STEM helpers (use for steps, not shortcuts)</h2>
<p>Tools like Photomath, Microsoft Math Solver, Symbolab-style apps, and Wolfram’s free layers help when you:</p>
<ul>
<li>Attempt the problem first</li>
<li>Compare steps</li>
<li>Re-solve a similar problem cold</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid screenshot → copy → submit. Teachers can usually tell — and you won’t remember it.</p>
<h2>6) Notes → flashcards (free stack)</h2>
<ol>
<li>Paste lecture notes into ChatGPT/Claude</li>
<li>Generate 20 Q&#x26;A flashcards</li>
<li>Import into Anki (or any free flashcard app)</li>
<li>Review daily for 10 minutes</li>
</ol>
<pre><code class="language-text">Create 20 flashcards from my notes.
Format: Front | Back
One fact per card. No yes/no questions.
Notes:
{{paste}}
</code></pre>
<h2>Academic integrity (non-negotiable)</h2>
<ul>
<li>Follow your school/university AI policy</li>
<li>Use AI to <strong>learn</strong>, not to fake authorship</li>
<li>Cite sources properly</li>
<li>Expect oral follow-ups — if you can’t explain it, you didn’t learn it</li>
<li>Don’t paste confidential personal data into random sites</li>
</ul>
<p>Model choice context: <a href="/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini/">ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini</a>.</p>
<h2>A free homework workflow that beats “just Google it”</h2>
<h3>For problem sets (25–40 min)</h3>
<ol>
<li>Try 2 problems alone (10 min)</li>
<li>Ask AI to explain the <em>method</em> for one stuck problem (5 min)</li>
<li>Solve 2 new similar problems without AI (10–15 min)</li>
<li>Mark mistakes and save a “weak topics” note (5 min)</li>
</ol>
<h3>For reading / essays (40–60 min)</h3>
<ol>
<li>Read the assigned text yourself</li>
<li>AI: outline <em>your</em> thesis options (not a full essay)</li>
<li>You write the draft</li>
<li>AI: critique clarity and structure</li>
<li>You revise and cite</li>
</ol>
<h2>What usually does <em>not</em> work</h2>
<ul>
<li>Sites that only sell “finished homework”</li>
<li>Blind trust in AI citations</li>
<li>Ten apps at once (pick two: one chat + one practice)</li>
<li>Pasting the entire copyrighted textbook when policy forbids it</li>
<li>Skipping retrieval practice</li>
</ul>
<p>Protect focus while studying: <a href="/blog/attention-budget-deep-work/">The Attention Budget</a> · <a href="/blog/ai-time-management-apps/">AI Time Management Apps</a>.</p>
<h2>Free vs paid — when to upgrade</h2>
<p>Stay free if you study a few times a week and limits rarely block you.<br>
Upgrade only when you hit caps <strong>during real study sessions</strong> and the tool clearly raises quiz scores or clarity.</p>
<h2>Closing checklist</h2>
<ul class="contains-task-list">
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> One primary free chat tutor chosen</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Explain → practice → check loop tested</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> School AI policy reviewed</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Flashcard habit started (optional but powerful)</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> No unedited AI submissions</li>
</ul>
<p>Free AI homework helpers work when they act like a <strong>tutor</strong>, not an answer vending machine. Start with one chat tool, force yourself to attempt work first, and measure progress with quizzes you can pass cold.</p>
