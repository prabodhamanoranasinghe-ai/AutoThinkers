17:T1c92,<p>ChatGPT is useful for school only when your prompts are <strong>specific</strong>. Vague asks like “explain biology” create fluff. Tight prompts create quizzes, plans, and feedback you can actually study from.</p>
<p>This is a <strong>copy-paste prompt library</strong> for students — pair it with academic integrity rules from your school.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Related deep guide: <a href="/blog/ai-for-students-study-smarter/">AI for Students: How to Study Smarter…</a></p>
</blockquote>
<h2>How to use these prompts</h2>
<ol>
<li>Paste the prompt into ChatGPT</li>
<li>Replace <code>{{brackets}}</code></li>
<li>Ask for a second pass: “Make this harder” or “Mark my answers”</li>
<li>Study the output — do not submit unedited AI writing as your own</li>
</ol>
<h2>1) Study planner prompt</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: academic coach for busy students.
Objective: build a realistic study plan.
Course: {{course}}
Exam/deadline: {{date}}
Available study time: {{hours per day}} on {{days}}
Current level: {{beginner/intermediate/struggling}}
Constraints:
- Include active recall and short breaks
- Do not schedule more than I can actually do
Output:
1. Weekly schedule table
2. Daily 45-minute session template
3. Priority topics list
4. What to cut if I fall behind
</code></pre>
<h2>2) Lecture / notes summary prompt</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: careful study tutor.
Objective: turn my notes into a revision sheet.
Subject: {{subject}}
Notes:
{{paste notes}}
Constraints:
- Keep key terms and definitions
- Flag anything that looks incomplete
- Do not invent facts not in my notes
Output:
1. One-page summary
2. Key terms list
3. 8 practice questions
4. 3 likely exam traps
</code></pre>
<h2>3) Explain-it-simply prompt (Feynman style)</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: patient tutor.
Objective: explain {{topic}} so I can teach it back.
My level: {{level}}
Constraints:
- Use a simple analogy
- Then a precise version
- Ask me 3 check questions at the end
Output:
Analogy → Core explanation → Common mistakes → Check questions
</code></pre>
<p>After it asks questions, answer <strong>without looking</strong>, then paste your answers for marking.</p>
<h2>4) Practice quiz generator</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: exam coach.
Objective: generate a practice quiz on {{topic}}.
Difficulty: {{easy/medium/hard}}
Source material:
{{paste notes or textbook section summary}}
Constraints:
- Mix multiple choice and short answer
- Include an answer key separately
- Mark which questions test memorization vs application
Output:
Quiz (10 questions)
Then: Answer key + short explanations
</code></pre>
<h2>5) Essay outline prompt (not a full ghostwritten essay)</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: writing coach.
Objective: help me outline MY essay.
Prompt/assignment: {{paste assignment}}
My thesis idea: {{your thesis}}
Evidence I already have: {{bullets}}
Constraints:
- Do not write the full essay
- Strengthen structure and argument flow
- Suggest where I need sources
Output:
1. Thesis refinement options (2)
2. Outline with H2/H3
3. Evidence gaps
4. Counterargument section
</code></pre>
<p>Use AI for structure. Write the final prose yourself unless your syllabus explicitly allows otherwise.</p>
<h2>6) Weak-area drill prompt</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: tutor who targets weak spots.
I keep missing questions about: {{weak topic}}
Show what I got wrong:
{{paste mistake or confused explanation}}
Constraints:
- Diagnose the misunderstanding
- Give 5 progressive drills
- End with a mini-quiz
</code></pre>
<h2>7) Flashcard maker prompt</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: spaced-repetition coach.
Objective: create flashcards from my notes.
Notes:
{{paste}}
Constraints:
- One fact per card
- Prefer “question → answer” format
- Avoid yes/no cards
Output:
20 flashcards in a two-column list (Front | Back)
</code></pre>
<h2>8) Oral exam / presentation rehearsal</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text">Role: examiner.
Topic: {{topic}}
Ask me 8 questions one at a time.
After each answer I paste, score me 1–5 and tell me what was missing.
Start with question 1 only.
</code></pre>
<h2>Prompt upgrades that instantly improve results</h2>
<p>Add these lines to almost any student prompt:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>Do not invent citations.</code></li>
<li><code>If information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first.</code></li>
<li><code>Keep answers concise; prefer bullets and tables.</code></li>
<li><code>Challenge me — do not overpraise.</code></li>
</ul>
<p>For prompt structure fundamentals, see the <a href="/blog/prompt-craft-tutorial-real-work/">Prompt Craft Tutorial</a>.</p>
<h2>Academic integrity (non-negotiable)</h2>
<ul>
<li>Follow your institution’s AI policy</li>
<li>Cite sources properly</li>
<li>Use AI to <strong>learn</strong>, not to fake authorship</li>
<li>Expect oral follow-ups in many courses — if you cannot explain it, you did not learn it</li>
</ul>
<h2>7-day prompt challenge</h2>
<ul>
<li>Day 1: Study planner</li>
<li>Day 2: Notes → revision sheet</li>
<li>Day 3: 10-question quiz + self-mark</li>
<li>Day 4: Essay outline only</li>
<li>Day 5: Weak-area drills</li>
<li>Day 6: Flashcards</li>
<li>Day 7: Oral rehearsal</li>
</ul>
<p>Track what improved: quiz scores, recall speed, or clarity when explaining out loud.</p>
<h2>Closing checklist</h2>
<ul class="contains-task-list">
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Saved 5 prompts you will reuse</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> One course selected for this week</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Quiz + answer-key workflow tested</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Integrity rules reviewed</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Primary study model chosen (<a href="/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini/">ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini</a>)</li>
</ul>
<p>Great prompts turn ChatGPT into a tutor. Weak prompts turn it into a distraction.</p>
