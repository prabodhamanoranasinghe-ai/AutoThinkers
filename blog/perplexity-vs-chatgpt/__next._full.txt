<p>Perplexity shines as an AI-powered research/search layer. ChatGPT shines as a general workbench for writing, reasoning, coding help, and custom workflows. Many operators keep both — but you should pick a default.</p>

<h2>Perplexity: best for research with a paper trail</h2>
<p><strong>Choose Perplexity when you want:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Fast overviews with sources you can open and verify</li>
<li>Comparison questions (“X vs Y for small teams”)</li>
<li>A search-like loop: ask → scan citations → refine</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Watch outs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Sources can be uneven; <strong>click through</strong> before you trust a claim</li>
<li>Not always the best home for multi-step creative projects</li>
<li>Still can miss context or over-weight weak pages</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> students, analysts, founders doing market/tool research, writers collecting evidence.</p>
<p><strong>Research prompt pattern:</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-text">Question: {{topic}}
Audience: {{who}}
Constraints:
- Separate facts, claims, and unknowns
- List sources I should verify first
- Flag anything that looks outdated
Output:
1. Short answer
2. Key points with source links
3. What to double-check manually
</code></pre>
<h2>ChatGPT: best everyday AI workbench</h2>
<p><strong>Choose ChatGPT when you want:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Outlines, drafts, rewrites, and critique loops</li>
<li>Prompt libraries and repeatable workflows</li>
<li>Coding help, tutoring, and multimodal drafts (varies by plan)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Watch outs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Can sound confident while wrong — especially on niche facts</li>
<li>“Search-like” answers still need verification</li>
<li>Easy to skip sources if you don’t ask for them</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> creators, operators, students writing/studying, anyone building a personal AI habit.<br>
Prompt craft: <a href="/blog/prompt-craft-tutorial-real-work/">Prompt Craft Tutorial</a>.</p>
<h2>When Perplexity wins</h2>
<ul>
<li>“What happened / what changed recently?”</li>
<li>“Best tools for X — with sources”</li>
<li>Literature-style scanning before you write</li>
<li>Competitive or product research with links to cite</li>
</ul>
<h2>When ChatGPT wins</h2>
<ul>
<li>Turning notes into a tutorial, email, or script</li>
<li>Multi-step editing (“shorter, sharper, keep my voice”)</li>
<li>Study drills and practice quizzes (<a href="/blog/ai-for-students-study-smarter/">AI for Students</a>)</li>
<li>Building reusable prompts and SOPs</li>
</ul>
<h2>The winning combo (15–40 minutes)</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Perplexity (research):</strong> collect claims + sources (10–15 min)</li>
<li><strong>Human gate:</strong> open the top 3–5 links; discard weak ones (5–10 min)</li>
<li><strong>ChatGPT (draft):</strong> write the artifact from <em>your</em> verified notes (15 min)</li>
</ol>
<pre><code class="language-text">Using ONLY the notes below, write {{artifact}}.
Do not invent stats or citations.
If something is missing, ask up to 3 questions.
Notes:
{{paste verified bullets + links}}
</code></pre>
<p>This is the same “verify then ship” discipline behind <a href="/blog/ai-content-workflow-60-minutes/">AI Content Workflow in 60 Minutes</a>.</p>
<h2>Students &#x26; knowledge workers</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use <strong>Perplexity</strong> to map a topic and find readings</li>
<li>Use <strong>ChatGPT</strong> to quiz yourself, outline essays, and explain hard parts</li>
<li>Never submit unedited AI text as your own work</li>
</ul>
<p>More: <a href="/blog/best-chatgpt-prompts-for-students/">Best ChatGPT Prompts for Students</a>.</p>
<h2>Small businesses</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Perplexity:</strong> vendor comparisons, policy scanning, market questions</li>
<li><strong>ChatGPT:</strong> customer replies, SOPs, marketing drafts<br>
See <a href="/blog/how-small-businesses-can-use-chatgpt/">How Small Businesses Can Use ChatGPT</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pricing mindset</h2>
<ul>
<li>Pay for <strong>Perplexity</strong> if research with sources is weekly and free limits block you</li>
<li>Pay for <strong>ChatGPT</strong> if it’s your daily drafting/thinking home</li>
<li>Paying for both is reasonable for research-heavy creators — wasteful if one is idle</li>
</ul>
<p>Review with your stack habits from <a href="/blog/best-ai-productivity-tools/">Best AI Productivity Tools</a>.</p>
<h2>20-minute decision test</h2>
<p>Run both on:</p>
<ol>
<li>A <strong>research</strong> question in your niche (score sources + usefulness)</li>
<li>A <strong>rewrite</strong> of your own rough paragraph</li>
<li>A <strong>plan</strong> (“turn these notes into a checklist”)</li>
</ol>
<p>Keep the winner for each job. Default to one app on your dock.</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ol>
<li>Treating Perplexity citations as automatically correct</li>
<li>Using ChatGPT as a search engine without verification</li>
<li>Drafting long posts before gathering sources</li>
<li>Buying both on day one without a weekly workflow</li>
<li>Pasting confidential data into the wrong tool/plan</li>
</ol>
<h2>Closing checklist</h2>
<ul class="contains-task-list">
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Default tool chosen (Perplexity or ChatGPT)</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Research → verify → draft pipeline tested once</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> “No invented citations” rule in your prompts</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> One real weekly use case attached</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Second tool optional for the other job</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> Use <strong>Perplexity</strong> to find and frame what’s true. Use <strong>ChatGPT</strong> to turn verified thinking into work products. If you only buy one first, pick based on whether your bottleneck is <strong>research</strong> or <strong>drafting</strong>.</p>
